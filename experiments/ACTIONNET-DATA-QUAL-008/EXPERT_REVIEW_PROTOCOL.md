# Expert review protocol

## Admission

Experts may self-apply, but cannot self-verify. A profile becomes routable only after identity, credential, domain, specialty, jurisdiction, ethics/conflict, and calibration checks are complete. Credentials expire and must be revalidated.

## Deterministic routing

A case is eligible for an expert only when all hard gates pass:

- verified profile;
- current credential;
- calibration score of at least 0.80;
- verified domain and at least one required specialty;
- verified jurisdiction;
- current conflict attestation;
- tenant permission for institution-originated cases;
- no self-review.

Eligible experts are ranked deterministically. The router records accepted assignments and rejection reasons. It does not infer credentials from biography text.

## Review depth

- Moderate risk: one independent review; adjudicate disagreement.
- High risk: two independent reviews; adjudicate disagreement.
- Critical risk: two independent reviews, required adjudicator, and required safety review.

The candidate author cannot be final adjudicator. Reviewers cannot review their own submissions. Ordinary support staff cannot create safety labels.

## Review output

The reviewer must submit a decision, failed-condition set, evidence references, confidence, conflict attestation, and any corrected post-state. Free text may explain the result but is not itself the label.

## Training eligibility

Expert agreement is necessary but insufficient. Tenant permission, source permission, privacy review, de-identification, duplicate checks, protected-set exclusion, dataset freeze, and release approval must also pass.
